## Context to Share Before the Call

Send the one-pager or these bullet points so they have context:

- **What Docita is:** Clinic management OS for Indian private clinics and hospitals — appointments, patient records, consultations, prescriptions, billing, WhatsApp communication, all in one connected workspace.
- **Stage:** Production-ready, live, 3,100+ tests passing, 47 clinician routes, 16 admin routes, zero type/lint errors.
- **Target users:** Solo doctors, front desk staff, clinic owners, hospital admins.
- **Pricing:** 5 tiers (Starter → Enterprise), each with Full or Front Desk mode. 30-day free trial, no credit card.
- **Current marketing:** Almost none. We have 164 video scripts drafted but not yet produced. No paid ads, no outbound, no content pipeline running yet.
- **What we want:** Strategic guidance on positioning, channels, early wins, and content priorities.

## Part 1: Positioning & Messaging (10 min)

### Primary Questions

**Q1. How should we position Docita in the Indian clinic software market?**

> Context: The market has Practo (patient-facing, not clinic ops), ClinicCloud (Australian, not India-specific), and a long tail of local tools that are mostly Excel + WhatsApp. We want to own "connected clinic operations" — not just appointments, not just billing.

- Follow-up: Is "healthcare operating layer" the right framing, or is that too abstract for clinic owners?
- Follow-up: Should we lead with a specific pain point (e.g., "stop losing patients because follow-ups fall through the cracks") or with the full platform story?

**Q2. What's the single most compelling message for an Indian clinic owner who's never heard of us?**

> Context: Our current messaging is "Run your clinic from one place." We also have "The Bridge from Paper to Cloud."

- Follow-up: Does "start with one doctor, one front desk user" lower the barrier enough, or does it undersell the product?
- Follow-up: Should we lead with the free trial or with the problem/solution?

**Q3. How do we differentiate from Practo specifically?**

> Context: Practo is patient-facing (patients find doctors). Docita is clinic-facing (clinics run operations). But many clinic owners conflate the two.

- Follow-up: Is there a risk of being compared to Practo and losing on brand recognition? How do we preempt that?
- Follow-up: Should we explicitly say "We are not Practo" or let the product speak?

---

## Part 2: Channels & Distribution (10 min)

### Primary Questions

**Q4. What marketing channels actually work for reaching Indian clinic owners and solo doctors?**

> Context: Our assumption is LinkedIn (for clinic owners/admins), WhatsApp groups (for doctors), medical conferences, and doctor communities. But we have no data.

- Follow-up: Is Google Ads / Facebook Ads worth testing early, or is that a money pit for this audience?
- Follow-up: What about YouTube — is a product demo channel viable for clinic software in India?

**Q5. What's the most cost-effective channel to get our first 50 clinics?**

> Context: We're bootstrapped / early-stage. Limited budget. Need proof of concept before scaling spend.

- Follow-up: Should we focus on one geography first (e.g., Hyderabad, our base) or go national from day one?
- Follow-up: Is direct outreach (LinkedIn DMs, WhatsApp messages to clinic owners) viable, or does it feel spammy?

**Q6. Are there doctor/clinic communities or forums we should be present in?**

> Context: We know about Indian Medical Association (IMA) local chapters, doctor WhatsApp groups, and platforms like Sermo. But we don't know which ones actually convert.

- Follow-up: How do you approach a doctor community without being "just another sales pitch"?
- Follow-up: Should we sponsor or participate in medical conferences, or is that premature?

---

## Part 3: Content Strategy (5 min)

### Primary Questions

**Q7. We have 164 video scripts ready (product demos + educational healthcare content). Should we produce these first, or focus on other channels?**

> Context: The scripts cover product walkthroughs (long-form + short-form), healthy living, nutrition, Indian healthcare issues, mental health, doctor wellness, clinic technology, DPDP compliance, and ABDM compliance.

- Follow-up: Should we prioritize product demo videos or educational content first?
- Follow-up: What's the minimum viable content cadence — 1 post/week, 3/week, daily?

**Q8. Should we build a blog / SEO content engine, or is that a long game we can't afford yet?**

> Context: We could produce articles on clinic management, billing best practices, DPDP compliance for clinics, etc.

- Follow-up: Are there specific SEO keywords in the Indian clinic software space worth ranking for?
- Follow-up: Is LinkedIn articles / newsletter a better fit than a standalone blog?

---

## Part 4: Quick Wins & First 90 Days (5 min)

### Primary Questions

**Q9. If you were advising Docita with a limited budget, what 2-3 things would you do in the first 90 days?**

- Follow-up: Which of these has the fastest feedback loop (signups, demo requests, content engagement)?
- Follow-up: What metrics should we track to know if something is working?

**Q10. What's the biggest mistake early-stage clinic software startups make in marketing?**

- Follow-up: Is "building in public" viable for healthcare software, or does it raise compliance/trust concerns?
- Follow-up: Should we seek testimonials / case studies from early users, or wait until we have more traction?

---

## Part 5: Objections & Positioning Risks (Optional, if time allows)

**Q11. What objections do clinic owners typically raise against switching from paper/Excel/WhatsApp?**

> Our current objection handling:
> - "We already use WhatsApp" → "Docita connects WhatsApp to the patient record so nothing falls through."
> - "We're too small" → "Start with one doctor. Scale when ready."
> - "My staff won't learn it" → "Set up takes 5 minutes. Front desk sees value on day one."

- Follow-up: Are we missing the most common objection?
- Follow-up: Should we address objections proactively in content, or handle them in sales conversations?

**Q12. Is there a risk of being seen as "just another clinic software" in a crowded market?**

- Follow-up: What would make someone remember Docita after seeing 3 clinic tools in a week?
- Follow-up: Is there a "hook" or viral mechanic that could work for clinic software?
