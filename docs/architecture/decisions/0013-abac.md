# 0013: Layered attribute-based access control on top of RBAC (ABAC-on-RBAC)

- **Status**: accepted
- **Date**: 2026-08-06
- **Deciders**: platform
- **Tags**: auth, security, compliance

## Context

The object-level access inventory in [`ABAC_MIGRATION_PLAN.md`](../../analysis/ABAC_MIGRATION_PLAN.md) found that the API's only object-level gates are the roles matrix (`@Roles` + `getDbRolesForTarget`), the tier/feature gates (`@RequireFeature`, `TierGuard`), tenant scoping inside repository queries, and RLS as the data-plane backstop. Attribute-based conditions are partially implemented but not enforced:

- `AuthorizationService` (with `ResourcePolicyRegistry` and the four legacy `ResourcePolicy` implementations in `apps/api/src/shared/modules/auth/authorization/`) exists but is **unwired** — no guard invokes it.
- `@DoctorPatientScoped` / `PatientDoctorGuard` is registered in `AuthModule` but **not applied to any live controller route**. Doctor-assignment pinning and PATIENT own-row enforcement exist nowhere on the route surface.
- PATIENT self-service reads (`/patient-portal/*`) are gated by `@Roles(Role.PATIENT)` at the controller level; the patient profile is derived from the JWT, not from a per-request resource policy.

The route surface therefore cannot express "object belongs to your clinic **and** you are assigned / you own the row" without a policy stage. Adding more `@Roles` entries does not scale (role explosion) and cannot encode resource attributes (owner, classification, consent state).

## Decision

Ship a **layered ABAC-on-RBAC** model where the existing guard chain stays in force and a new policy stage adds object-level conditions only:

1. **Guard order** (unchanged, then additive): JWT → tenant/context → roles matrix → subscription → tier/feature → **new `PolicyGuard`** (global `APP_GUARD`, no-op without metadata) → service/repository tenant scoping → RLS.
2. **Deny-overrides + default-deny.** The `PolicyGuard` denies (403, or 404 for `tenant_mismatch` to preserve the no-existence-disclosure wire contract) on anything but an explicit allow. A declared policy with no registered resolver is a misconfiguration and denies — never passes.
3. **Typed-policy engine in a new workspace package** `packages/authorization`: pure, framework-agnostic `AbacPolicy` implementations (`evaluatePolicy`, `denyOverrides`, builders) with no NestJS or Prisma imports. The API's `AuthorizationAction`/`AuthorizationDenyReason` become type aliases of the package vocabulary, keeping the outward contract stable.
4. **Zod-first attributes.** Subject/resource/environment attribute schemas live once in `packages/types/src/authorization/` and are the single source of truth; no parallel hand-written interfaces.
5. **Decorator-driven posture.** Routes declare `@RequirePolicy(resource, action)`; a static gate (`check:policy-resolver-coverage`, wired into `verify:standard`) fails the build if a declared resource has no registered resolver.
6. **No XACML.** Policies are typed TypeScript classes, not XML/JSON policy documents — the policy set is small and reviewed in PRs.
7. **UI remains non-authoritative.** A frontend permission snapshot (Phase C7 of the migration plan) is deferred to a later slice.
8. **RLS stays on** as the data-plane backstop; ABAC is defense-in-depth at the application layer, not a replacement.

## Deferred product decision: doctor assignment-pinning on the staff patients surface

`PatientAbacPolicy` implements assignment-pinning behind `resource.requiresAssignment` (default `false`). The staff resolver (`PatientResourceResolver`) keeps it `false` today because **assignment-pinning is not enforced anywhere on the live staff surface** — enforcing it silently would tighten clinician access without a product decision. Enabling it on the staff surface is a product decision that must land with a differential-equivalence pass and a separate PR (C1 phase 2 of the migration plan), never silently. The future patient-portal resolver flips `requiresAssignment` on for portal reads.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Pros | Cons | Why rejected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Extend the roles matrix only | No new machinery | Role explosion; cannot encode resource attributes (owner, classification, consent) | Does not satisfy the inventory requirements |
| Wire the legacy `AuthorizationService.can()` directly | Reuses existing code | Legacy policies are Nest-bound, not reusable by the worker/frontend snapshots; no attribute schemas | The package promotion satisfies the two-consumer rule and keeps policies pure |
| XACML/policy documents | Standards-driven | Heavyweight, stringly-typed, poor DX for a small policy set | Typed classes reviewed in PRs are simpler (KISS) |
| Enforce assignment-pinning now | Closes the gap immediately | Unauthorized behavior tightening on the staff surface | Requires a product decision + differential pass first |

## Consequences

- Positive: object-level conditions become expressible (`tenant + owner/assignment + classification`), the engine is reusable by the worker and future frontend snapshots, deny reasons are machine-readable, and the static gate catches resolver misconfigurations at CI time.
- Negative: a second enforcement stage adds surface area; every new `@RequirePolicy` route must register a resolver or the guard 403s (intended, fail-closed); attribute vocabulary must be versioned in `packages/types`.
- Follow-up work: C2 appointments, C3 prescriptions, C4 lab/payments, C5 audit-log resources, C6 cross-tenant referral, C7 frontend snapshot, Phase D retirement ledger, Phase E parity-gate cutover, worker-side consumption of `@workspace/authorization`.
