# Docita — Connector Partner Recruitment Roadmap

> How to find, qualify, and activate the people who can introduce Docita
> into Indian clinics and small hospitals.

This document is the operational counterpart to the high-level
positioning in [`MEETING_PREP_MARKETING_STRATEGY.md`](MEETING_PREP_MARKETING_STRATEGY.md).
Where that doc asks *what to do*, this one is *how to actually do it*,
week by week.

---

## 1. The model in one paragraph

Docita does not run its own field sales team. Instead, the founder recruits
"connectors" — people who already have trusted, recurring access to
Indian clinic owners and front desk staff. The connector mentions Docita
in the right conversation, hands over a free-pilot offer, and earns a
commission when the clinic converts. The founder does the demo,
migration, and onboarding personally.

This is the lowest-cost distribution channel available to a solo
founder because every connector is already being paid (or paid in
influence) to walk into clinics for *some other reason*. We are not
paying for that access; we are paying for the introduction.

The connector is **not** a salesperson. They are a referrer. The
founder closes.

## 2. Connector types, ranked by leverage

Not all connectors are equal. Start at the top and work down. Do not
run all tiers in parallel — one tier working beats five tiers noisy.

### Tier 1 — start here, first 30 days

| Connector type | Why they work | Typical relationship with clinics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pharma medical representatives (MRs)** | Already visit 50–100 doctors a month. Commission-based themselves, used to side income. See the prescription/follow-up pain daily. | Standing visit, often 5–10 min with the doctor, casual chat with staff. |
| **Reception / clinic management trainers** | Train clinic front desk staff; the receptionist is the actual champion inside the clinic. | Trusted advisor relationship; clinic owner listens when they speak. |
| **Local medical equipment / consumables suppliers** | Walk into clinics monthly for orders. Already discussing operational needs. | Quasi-vendor; trusted for purchases, neutral on software. |

### Tier 2 — start after Tier 1 is producing

| Connector type | Why they work | Typical relationship with clinics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CA / tax consultants serving doctors** | Doctors trust their CA more than any software vendor. CAs visit for quarterly filings. | Annual + quarterly visits, advisory. |
| **Diagnostic lab collection agents** | Often physically located inside clinics (collection centers). See workflow chaos first-hand. | Co-located daily; informal influence. |
| **Medical billing / transcription services** | Handle back-office for clinics; know the RCM pain. | Trusted vendor; consulted on operational changes. |

### Tier 3 — opportunistic, after 3+ case studies exist

| Connector type | Why they work | Typical relationship with clinics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hospital accreditation consultants (NABH / ABDM)** | Walk in for paid projects; clinic trusts them with sensitive decisions. | Project-based, high credibility. |
| **Micro-influencer doctors (YouTube / Instagram / LinkedIn)** | Have audiences of other doctors. Cheap mentions. | One-to-many, lower conversion but high volume. |
| **Insurance TPA agents** | Visit hospitals for cashless claims; chat with owners casually. | Periodic, administrative. |

Pharma MRs are the single highest-leverage connector for Docita. They
have warm daily access, are used to commission-only income, and have
no conflict of interest with clinic software. If only one tier is
built, build this one.

## 3. Where to find each tier

Concrete sources, search strings, and offline tactics. Run these in
parallel; the work is per-tier, not per-person.

### 3.1 Pharma MRs

- **LinkedIn search** (free):
  `("medical representative" OR "pharma sales" OR "MR") AND ("Hyderabad" OR "Telangana")`.
  Filter by `People` → `2nd` connections and below; these are the
  people most likely to reply to a cold DM.
- **JustDial / IndiaMART** directory: search `pharmaceutical distributors Hyderabad`
  → call each, ask for their field reps in the local territory.
- **Pharma company local offices** (Cipla, Sun, Mankind, Alkem, Torrent
  Hyderabad branches). Walk in or call the HR/admin. Ask which reps
  cover which pin codes. Many will share territory lists informally.
- **Offline:** hospital-cluster parking lots in the morning. MRs park
  near clinics between 9–11 AM. The "I saw your car outside Dr X's
  clinic" opener is brutally effective.

### 3.2 Reception trainers

- **IMA Telangana** (`ima-telangana.org` or local chapter contacts).
  Ask for the receptionist training program leads.
- **Google search:** `clinic receptionist training Hyderabad`,
  `front office management course Hyderabad medical`.
- **YouTube:** search `clinic receptionist Hindi` or `OPD billing
  training` — trainers who run paid cohorts usually list contact info
  in the description.
- **LinkedIn:** `clinic trainer` / `medical receptionist trainer`.

### 3.3 Medical equipment / consumables suppliers

- **IndiaMART:** search `hospital equipment supplier Hyderabad`,
  `clinic consumables Hyderabad`. Each supplier has 3–10 field reps.
- **JustDial:** same keywords.
- **Offline:** visit a clinic-dense area (Kukatpally, Dilsukhnagar,
  Secunderabad). Each clinic has 1–2 supplier cards at reception.
  Photograph them, call the next day.
- **Trade shows:** Medical Fair India (Mumbai, annual), Indian
  Diagnostic Expo. Even without attending, exhibitor lists are
  searchable and can be cold-emailed.

### 3.4 CA / tax consultants

- **ICAI directory:** `icai.org` → `Members' Directory` → filter by
  city and specialization. Look for firms listing "medical
  professionals" or "doctors" in their client list.
- **JustDial:** search `chartered accountant doctors Hyderabad` and
  similar.
- **Existing doctor customers:** once you have 2–3 clinics on Docita,
  ask the owner directly who does their taxes. One warm ask beats
  fifty cold calls.

### 3.5 Lab collection agents

- **Dr. Lal PathLabs, SRL, Thyrocare, Apollo Diagnostics, Vijaya,
  local labs:** each franchisee has 2–5 field agents covering clinics.
  Call the Hyderabad regional office and ask for the partnership /
  franchise desk.
- **Google Maps:** search `pathology lab near [clinic-dense area]` →
  call each, ask who covers which clinics.
- **Clinic reception:** the staff already know the lab agent by name.
  One question: "Who is your lab collection agent?" gets you a name
  and a phone number in 10 seconds.

### 3.6 NABH / ABDM consultants

- **NABH portal:** `nabh.co` lists accredited assessor firms. Call
  their Hyderabad office; many are willing to refer for side income.
- **ABDM ecosystem:** the ABDM integrator partner list (NHA website)
  shows consulting firms already selling to clinics.
- **LinkedIn:** `NABH consultant Hyderabad`, `ABDM consultant`.

### 3.7 Micro-influencer doctors

- **YouTube:** search `clinic management India`, `doctor vlog India`,
  `practice tips Telugu` — flag channels with 1k–50k subscribers
  (engagement sweet spot, affordable).
- **Instagram:** hashtags `#indianDoctor`, `#telugudoctor`,
  `#doctordiaries`.
- **LinkedIn:** doctors with 500–5,000 followers who post weekly.

### 3.8 Insurance TPA agents

- **IRDAI-registered TPA list** (`irdai.gov.in`).
- **Hospital billing department:** ask the front desk which TPAs they
  deal with most. Call the TPA regional office.

## 4. Qualifying a connector

Before paying anyone anything, run a 10-minute qualification call.
Three questions determine whether to proceed:

1. **Access:** "How many clinics do you currently visit in a typical
   month?" Anything below 10 is not worth the time. Above 30 is
   ideal.
2. **Relationship depth:** "When you walk in, are you seeing the
   doctor directly, or mostly the reception?" Direct doctor access is
   gold; reception-only is workable. If neither, drop them.
3. **Motivation:** "Are you open to a small side income based on
   clinics you refer?" If they hesitate or want a salary, they will
   behave like an employee. You want partners, not hires.

Red flags — drop the conversation politely:

- Wants a salary or fixed monthly payment before any clinic converts.
- Has no current clinic access (claims "connections" without proof).
- Pushes for exclusivity ("only I bring you clinics"). Premature.
- Doesn't ask a single question about the product. Anyone serious about
  referring your tool asks what it does.

Green flags — proceed:

- Asks about pricing, onboarding flow, or which clinics it suits best.
- Offers to bring the founder along on their next visit.
- Mentions specific clinic names where they think it would fit.
- Has done referral work before and mentions how the previous
  arrangement worked.

## 5. The offer structure

The offer is what makes the connector strategy work. Wrong offer = ghost.
Right offer = the connector now has a reason to bring you up
naturally.

### 5.1 The base offer (use this for almost everyone)

- **Retainer:** ₹0 for the first 2 months. (Don't pay until they
  deliver.)
- **Free-pilot offer they bring to clinics:** "60 days free, full
  migration included, no credit card, no commitment. After 60 days,
  the clinic pays ₹999/month or walks away."
- **Connector commission on conversion:** ₹1,500 per clinic that
  converts to a paid plan after the pilot.
- **Bonus:** ₹500 extra if the clinic stays paid for 90 days. Filters
  out quick-churn referrals.

Total cost per converted clinic: ~₹2,000. At a ₹999/month plan with
60% annual retention, that's roughly 2 months of revenue per clinic to
acquire. For a bootstrapped founder with no ad budget, this is
acceptable.

### 5.2 Tiered offer (for connectors who deliver consistently)

After 3 successful referrals, upgrade them:

- Retainer: ₹5,000/month for ongoing relationship.
- Commission: ₹2,000 per conversion, ₹1,000 90-day bonus.
- Quarterly bonus: ₹5,000 if they hit 5 conversions in the quarter.

This converts a hit-run referrer into a real channel partner. The
retainer filters for seriousness; the bonus keeps them motivated past
the 3rd clinic.

### 5.3 What to *never* offer

- Equity. Premature for a connector relationship; will not match the
  effort they actually put in and creates legal complexity.
- Salary. They are not your employee. Salary converts the
  relationship into a management problem.
- Exclusivity. Connectors talk to multiple tools. Exclusive deals
  fail because the connector has nothing to compare you against
  internally and loses motivation when offers dry up.

## 6. Outreach scripts

Three channels. Use all three. WhatsApp is the highest-converting for
India.

### 6.1 WhatsApp first message (use for all Tier 1 / Tier 2 connectors)

Keep it under 80 words. Lead with the offer, not the product.

> Hi [Name], I'm [Your name], founder at Docita — clinic management
> software for Indian clinics. I'm not selling anything to you directly.
> I have a free 60-day pilot offer for clinics, and I pay ₹1,500 per
> clinic that converts after the pilot.
>
> If you visit clinics in your regular work and this seems like a fit
> for 2-3 of them, can we have a 10-min call this week? I'll explain
> the offer, you decide.
>
> No pressure either way.

What this does:

- Opens by clarifying *you* are not the buyer.
- States the money in the first 2 lines.
- Asks for a small commitment (a call), not a sale.
- Removes pressure, which paradoxically makes them more likely to
  reply.

### 6.2 LinkedIn DM (for connectors you can't reach on WhatsApp)

Similar structure. Mention one specific thing — their company, a
recent post, the area they cover — so it doesn't read as a copy-paste.

> Hi [Name], saw you're covering [area/specialty] — I work with Indian
> clinics on operations software and I'm building a small partner
> network of people who already visit clinics for other work. Quick
> call this week to see if it's a fit?

### 6.3 First call script (10 minutes, structured)

Run this verbatim. Don't freestyle.

> 1. (1 min) "Tell me about your current work — what do you do, how
>    many clinics do you see in a typical month?"
> 2. (2 min) Listen. Take notes. Don't pitch yet.
> 3. (3 min) Explain Docita in 3 sentences: "It's a clinic operating
>    system — appointments, EMR, prescriptions, WhatsApp, billing —
>    built for Indian private clinics. ₹999/month after a free pilot.
>    We do the migration ourselves."
> 4. (2 min) Explain the offer: free pilot, ₹1,500 per conversion,
>    no retainer to start.
> 5. (2 min) "Does this fit your network? Are there 2-3 clinics you
>    could see this being useful for?"
> 6. Close: "If yes, I'll send you a one-pager you can share or
>    mention. If not, no worries, thank you for the time."

If they say yes, send the one-pager (`docs/DOCITA_ONE_PAGER.md`) and
agree on the first clinic within 7 days. If no clinic in 14 days,
they're not going to deliver. Move on.

### 6.4 The one-pager the connector shares

Use the existing [`DOCITA_ONE_PAGER.md`](DOCITA_ONE_PAGER.md) for
email. For WhatsApp-shareable, create a 4-bullet version:

> - 60-day free pilot, full migration by our team
> - Appointments + EMR + prescriptions + WhatsApp + billing in one app
> - After pilot: ₹999/month, no long-term contract
> - Book a 20-min demo: [link]

That's it. The connector shares this verbatim with 2-3 clinics. The
clinic books the demo. The founder runs it. The connector doesn't
demo — that's the line that keeps the model clean.

## 7. Tracking

Don't run this without a tracker. A Google Sheet is enough at this
stage. Columns:

| Name | Type (MR / trainer / supplier / etc.) | Source (LinkedIn / JustDial / referral) | First contact date | Channel (WA / LinkedIn / call) | Status (New / Qualified / Active / Inactive / Dropped) | Active clinics in network | Clinics referred | Clinics converted | Total earned | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Update weekly. The status field is the most important — it forces a
decision. Anything not updated in 14 days should be marked `Inactive`
or `Dropped`.

When you have 5+ active connectors, graduate to a real CRM. Until
then, the sheet is faster and less likely to die from setup
overhead.

## 8. The 90-day plan

### Days 1–7 — setup

- Build the Google Sheet tracker.
- Draft the WhatsApp + LinkedIn scripts (Section 6) — finalise them
  in your own voice, not generic.
- Pick the geographic radius. Default: 25 km around your Hyderabad
  address. List the top 10 clinic-dense pin codes.
- Set the conversion target: 3 paid clinics by day 90, 10 by day 180.
  This is realistic for a solo founder. Anything higher is a stretch;
  don't plan for it.

### Days 8–30 — Tier 1 only, find 20 connectors

- Run Section 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 in parallel.
- Goal: 20 connectors in the tracker. Expect 5 to qualify, 3 to
  become active.
- Daily time budget: 1.5 hours/day on connector outreach. Not more.
  Past that, response quality drops.
- Founder still does 2 in-person clinic visits per week (the
  fallback channel from the previous plan).

### Days 31–60 — activate Tier 1, start Tier 2

- The 3 active Tier 1 connectors should each have brought 1+ clinic
  into the pilot. Help them close. If a clinic sits in pilot without
  converting, the founder runs a "save" call.
- Start Section 3.4 and 3.5. Goal: +10 Tier 2 connector leads,
  +3 active.
- First 1–2 case studies land this month. Use them in the
  one-pager and in the next batch of outreach messages.

### Days 61–90 — optimise + add Tier 3

- Cut connectors who haven't delivered. Hard. Drop the bottom 50%
  of the active list. The remaining 50% are your real channel.
- Upgrade 2–3 best performers to the tiered offer (Section 5.2).
- Start Section 3.6 / 3.7 only if Tier 1 is producing reliably.
- Re-evaluate: are you at 3+ converted clinics? If yes, the model
  works. Scale. If no, the problem is in the *offer* or the
  *demo*, not the connector list.

### 90-day success criteria

- 30 connectors contacted
- 8–10 active connectors (1+ clinic referred each)
- 3+ clinics in paid plan
- 1 case study with named clinic + photo + measurable result
- 1 connector upgraded to retainer model

Anything less is signal that the model needs adjustment, not
abandonment. Anything more is bonus — don't scale on a sample size of
one good month.

## 9. What to do when a connector ghosts

It will happen. The script above is designed to reduce it, not
eliminate it. When it does:

1. **Wait 5 days** after the last message. Don't chase immediately.
2. **Send one follow-up, short:** "Hi [Name], just checking if you had
   a chance to think about it. If the timing isn't right, no
   problem — happy to revisit in a few weeks."
3. **If no reply in 5 more days, mark Inactive in the sheet.** Move
   on. Do not send a third message.

The temptation to chase harder is the wrong instinct. Chasing signals
desperation, which makes the connector discount your offer. Move to
the next name on the list. The model is built for attrition.

## 10. Common mistakes to avoid

- **Skipping the qualification call.** Anyone who asks to start
  without a call is going to misunderstand the offer and ghost.
  Always do the 10-minute call.
- **Asking the connector to demo.** They will do a bad demo because
  they don't know the product. You demo. They introduce.
- **Paying commission before the clinic converts.** Ties up cash and
  attracts the wrong people.
- **Onboarding the clinic yourself via the connector.** The
  connector introduces; the founder runs the demo, migration, and
  training. This is non-negotiable for the first 6 months. Founders
  who delegate onboarding too early lose visibility into what
  actually converts clinics.
- **Tracking connectors as if they were sales hires.** They're
  partners, not employees. Different metrics, different review
  cadence. A connector who refers 1 clinic in 60 days is fine; a
  sales hire with the same output is failing.
- **Promising exclusivity.** Premature. Comes up later, if at all.
