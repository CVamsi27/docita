# Clinic Interview Questions — Before / After Shots (YouTube + Instagram)

Question bank for filming **real clinics using Docita**. The goal of every interview is one thing: capture an honest **before vs after** — what the clinic's day looked like before Docita, and what changed. Use these to get usable soundbites, real numbers, and b-roll for Reels, Shorts, and longer YouTube testimonials.

> This is for *real* customer footage. It is different from the demo scripts (which use the Sunrise Health Clinic demo account). Here you are filming an actual clinic, real staff, real words — so consent and PHI rules are stricter. Read the Consent & compliance checklist first.

Related: viral formats and the SFX palette live in [`product-common.md`](product-common.md). The Before/After format there is the default edit shape for this footage.

---

## How to use this document

1. **Pick the role** you are interviewing — doctor, front desk, or owner — and use that track plus the universal questions.
2. **Always ask in before/after pairs.** First "how did you do this *before* Docita?", then "how do you do it *now*?". The contrast is the whole video.
3. **Chase the specifics.** "It's faster" is useless. "I used to spend the first hour copying yesterday's register; now I open one screen" is a clip. Follow every vague answer with "Can you give me a specific example?" or "How long did that take?".
4. **Get the numbers.** Minutes saved, patients per day, missed follow-ups before vs now, no-shows before vs now. Numbers make the thumbnail and the hook.
5. **Capture short, quotable lines** (see Soundbite prompts) — these become the on-screen text and the hook.
6. **Film the b-roll** listed at the end while they talk, so the editor has the "after" visuals to cut over.

Keep total interview to 20–30 minutes. You only need 3–5 strong before/after moments per clinic.

## Universal questions (any role)

**Setup / context**
- Tell us about your clinic — what kind of practice, how many doctors, how many patients on a busy day?
- How long have you been using Docita?
- Before Docita, what were you using to run the clinic? (Paper registers, Excel, WhatsApp, another tool?)

**The big before/after**
- Walk me through a typical morning *before* Docita. What was the first thing you did?
- Now walk me through that same morning *with* Docita. What's different?
- What's the one thing that used to frustrate you the most that you don't deal with anymore?
- If a friend running a clinic asked "should I bother switching?", what would you tell them?

**The honest take (keeps it credible)**
- What was the hardest part of switching? (Showing a real adjustment builds trust — don't cut all of it.)
- Is there anything you were skeptical about that turned out fine?

## Before/After by workflow area

Ask these as pairs. The left column is the "before" beat (often with a friction/negative SFX in the edit); the right is the Docita "after" (positive SFX).

### Appointments & the queue
- **Before:** How did patients book and how did you track who was waiting? What happened when it got busy?
- **After:** How does booking and the queue work now? What changed on a packed day?
- Follow-up for numbers: How many patients do you see on a busy day now vs before? Any change in no-shows?

### Patient records & history
- **Before:** When a returning patient came in, how did you find their history? How long did that take?
- **After:** How do you pull up a patient's full history now?
- Soundbite chase: Has there been a moment where having the history instantly actually changed the care you gave?

### Prescriptions
- **Before:** How were you writing prescriptions? Did patients ever struggle to read them, or lose them?
- **After:** How do you do prescriptions now? Do patients get them on WhatsApp?

### Billing & payments
- **Before:** How did you handle invoices and collecting payment? Did anything slip through — unbilled visits, partial payments forgotten?
- **After:** How does billing work now? Can you see what's pending vs paid?
- Numbers: Has it changed how much gets collected, or how fast?

### Follow-ups
- **Before:** How did you remember to follow up with patients? How many do you think slipped?
- **After:** How does Docita handle follow-ups now? (This is often the strongest "we were losing money/patients" story.)

### WhatsApp & patient communication
- **Before:** How were you messaging patients — reminders, reports, prescriptions? Personal phone? Manually?
- **After:** What does patient communication look like now?

### Owner visibility / reporting
- **Before:** At the end of the day, how did you know what happened — how many patients, how much billed, what's pending?
- **After:** How do you check on the clinic now? Can you see it from your phone / when you're away?

### Front desk / staff
- **Before:** What was the front desk juggling? How many things were open at once — registers, phone, WhatsApp, calculator?
- **After:** What does the front desk's screen look like now?
- Training chase: How long did it take a new staff member to learn the old way vs Docita?

## Role-specific tracks

### Doctor track
- What do you want when a patient walks in — and did the old way give it to you?
- Has Docita changed how much time you spend on admin vs actually with the patient?
- Is there a patient-care moment that's only possible because the record is all in one place?
- Be honest — does it slow you down anywhere, or has it stayed out of your way?

### Front desk / receptionist track
- What was the most stressful part of your day before? Is it still stressful?
- When the waiting room is full, what's different now?
- Have patients noticed anything change from their side?

### Clinic owner / hospital admin track
- What were you flying blind on before that you can now see?
- Has anything changed on the money side — collection, leakage, follow-up revenue?
- Was the switch worth it? What would you say to an owner sitting on the fence?
- For multi-doctor/hospital: did departments or doctors "talk" to each other before? Do they now?

## Numbers worth getting on camera

If they're comfortable sharing, ask for *before vs after* on any of these. One real number beats ten adjectives. Never pressure, never fabricate, and round/anonymise if they ask.

- Minutes saved per day (e.g. morning setup, finding a record, writing a prescription).
- Patients seen per day — before vs now.
- No-show rate — before vs now.
- Follow-ups that used to slip vs now.
- Time to train a new staff member.
- Anything they'd put a rupee figure on (uncollected/partial payments recovered, follow-up revenue).

> Treat any number as the clinic's claim, attributed to them — not a Docita guarantee. Don't generalise one clinic's result into "Docita saves every clinic X hours." Caption results as "Dr. ___, ___ Clinic" so it reads as a real person's experience.

## Soundbite prompts (the quotable lines)

Ask these near the end, when they're warmed up. You want one short, punchy sentence per prompt — these become the hook and the on-screen text.

- "Finish this: 'Before Docita, my day started with…'"
- "Finish this: 'The thing I don't miss is…'"
- "In one sentence, what's Docita to your clinic?"
- "What's the moment you realised this was actually working?"
- "If you had to go back to the old way for a week, what would happen?"

## B-roll shot list (film while they talk)

The interview gives you the audio; this gives the editor the "after" visuals to cut over. Shoot these at the real clinic, but **only frames with no patient data and no identifiable patients on screen** (see compliance).

- Wide of the clinic / reception / waiting area (establishing shot).
- Front desk staff using Docita on the screen — hands, clicks, the queue updating (over-the-shoulder, screen angled so no real names show, or use the demo account for the close-up).
- Doctor at the consultation screen (demo data on screen).
- A phone showing a WhatsApp message going out (demo number).
- The "before" props if they still have them: an old paper register, an Excel sheet, a stack of files — great for the before half of a split-screen.
- Hands, faces, expressions — the human texture between the screen shots.

> Tip: re-shoot any close-up of the screen on the **demo account** (Sunrise Health Clinic) so you get clean, PHI-free footage that matches the demo videos. Use the real clinic for atmosphere and the people; use demo data for anything where the screen is legible.

## Consent & compliance checklist

Real clinics, real people, real data — get this right before you hit record.

- [ ] **Signed video consent** from every person who appears on camera (doctor, staff) — covering use on YouTube, Instagram, and Docita marketing.
- [ ] **Clinic owner's written approval** to film on the premises and name the clinic.
- [ ] **No identifiable patients on camera** without their own explicit consent. Blur faces and name tags if any appear in the background.
- [ ] **No real patient data legible on any screen.** Use the demo account for legible screen close-ups; angle/blur any real screen.
- [ ] **No real phone numbers, addresses, ABHA/ABDM IDs, lab reports, or invoices** visible.
- [ ] Numbers shared by the clinic are attributed to them as their experience — never restated as a Docita guarantee or generalised claim.
- [ ] No medical advice or health claims in the cut (same guardrails as [`content-guidelines.md`](content-guidelines.md)).
- [ ] Give the clinic a **review/approval pass** on the final edit before publishing — it builds the relationship and avoids surprises.
