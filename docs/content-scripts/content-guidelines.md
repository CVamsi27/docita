# Content Guidelines for Docita Short-Form Healthcare Scripts

This document governs tone, compliance, editing, and reuse for every script in this folder.

## Purpose

These scripts build **brand authority** for Docita by providing genuine healthcare value to the audience. They are not product demos. The product connection is soft and occasional.

## Tone

### Use

- Plain, direct, educational language.
- "You" and "your" — speak to the viewer personally.
- Indian English where natural (cricket references, festival timing, local food examples).
- Confident but not preachy. Informed but not clinical.
- Founder voice when appropriate: honest, practical, no hype.

### Avoid

- Medical advice that replaces a doctor consultation.
- Fear-mongering or shock content.
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "guaranteed".
- Western-centric health examples when Indian examples work better (e.g., dal-chawal over quinoa bowls).
- Aggressive product CTAs. The product mention is a soft sign-off, not the point of the video.
- Emoji in scripts or on-screen text.

## Compliance Guardrails

1. **Never give medical advice.** Frame everything as general information, not a prescription. Always add "consult your doctor" where appropriate.
2. **Never show real patient data.** All screen recordings use demo data only.
3. **Do not make health claims.** Say "may help", "research suggests", "some studies show" — never "this cures" or "this prevents".
4. **Do not claim certifications or approvals.** Docita does not have NABL, ABDM certification, or similar unless explicitly stated elsewhere.
5. **Do not disparage competitors by name.** Talk about categories of problems, not specific products.
6. **Do not use trending copyrighted audio.** Use licensed music from YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay, or Uppbeat only.
7. **Include a small disclaimer on health-related videos:** "General information only. Consult a healthcare professional. Consult a healthcare professional."
8. **ABDM/ABHA references** should use "where enabled" and link to the sandbox setup guide if technical.

## Editing Rules

### Visual
- Record in 9:16 (1080x1920) for short-form.
- Use clean, bright footage. Natural light preferred.
- Show relevant visuals: food, exercise, clinic settings, app screens (demo only).
- No stock footage of hospitals or emergency rooms for general wellness content.
- Captions on every video. Clean sans-serif font. White text on dark strip.

### Audio
- Voiceover first, music second.
- Music at -24 to -30 LUFS under voice.
- Calm, modern, positive music.
- No trending copyrighted songs.

### Pacing
- Hook in the first 3 seconds. No warmup.
- One idea per shot. Fast but clean cuts.
- 2–3 second shots for short form.
- End with CTA in the last 3–5 seconds.

### Text Overlays
- Show 1–2 lines maximum at a time.
- Highlight one keyword per sentence in accent color.
- Never cover the subject's face or important visuals.
- Font: clean sans-serif, medium or semibold weight.

## Platform-Specific Notes

| Platform | Aspect Ratio | Max Length | Best Hook Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | 9:16 | 90s (60s preferred) | Question or surprising fact |
| YouTube Shorts | 9:16 | 60s | Direct statement or myth-bust |
| LinkedIn | 9:16 or 1:1 | 60s | Professional insight or stat |
| X (Twitter) | 16:9 or 1:1 | 45s | Provocative question or hot take |

## Content Calendar Rhythm

- **Monday:** Healthy living or fitness
- **Tuesday:** Nutrition or diet
- **Wednesday:** Indian healthcare issues
- **Thursday:** Patient education
- **Friday:** Mental health or doctor wellness
- **Saturday:** Clinic technology (soft product tie-in)
- **Sunday:** Repurpose best performer of the week into a new format

## Reuse Strategy

Each long script can be cut into 2–3 shorts by:
1. Isolating one tip or idea per short.
2. Using the hook text as the short's opening.
3. Keeping the same voiceover segment but trimming the middle.

Each short can be turned into:
- An Instagram carousel (text slides).
- A LinkedIn text post.
- An X thread.
- A blog paragraph for SEO.
